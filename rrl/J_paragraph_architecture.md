### 1.1 Mander, L. & Williams, H.T.P. (2024) "The robustness of some Carboniferous fossil leaf venation networks to simulated damage." *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 11:240086. DOI 10.1098/rsos.240086. **Open access; read in full via Europe PMC (PMC11076108).** Discussion = 5 paragraphs.

This is the closest structural analogue to your paper that exists: venation networks, simulated attack, robustness comparison across architectures, no physiology.

**Paragraph 3 ("congruent with intuition") — 10 sentences.**

| # | Function |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Claim fused with literature triangulation.** Headline result stated *as agreement* with three named evidence classes at once (intuition, theory, experiment), each cited, all in one sentence — not three sentences. |
| 2 | **Self-generated qualification.** Their own data contradict the clean version of the claim: the vulnerable class is not uniformly vulnerable. |
| 3 | **Geometric mechanism for the qualification.** Explains *why* one attack geometry spares the vulnerable class. |
| 4 | **External empirical realism check.** Palaeontological evidence that the sparing attack geometry is the one that actually occurred. |
| 5 | **Counter-qualification, bounded by literature.** The exception has an exception; published figures are cited as the counter-evidence. |
| 6 | **Return to own result, restated for the contrasting class.** |
| 7 | **Primary network mechanism, hedged** ("likely"): loops → redundancy → alternative paths. |
| 8 | **Secondary mechanism, more hedged** ("may also"): midrib/major veins. |
| 9 | **Mechanism 2 re-expressed in explicit network vocabulary**: contact points, redundancy, removal of a single point of failure. |
| 10 | **Third mechanism, flagged as unmeasured by this study, and delegated to literature.** Vein density is raised, explicitly disclaimed as not quantified here, then supported by a citation that supplies the measurement they lack. |

**Skeleton:** consistency-claim (with citations inline) → own counter-example → mechanism for the counter-example → external evidence that the counter-example is realistic → boundary on the counter-example → restate the contrasting class → mechanism 1 (hedged) → mechanism 2 (more hedged) → mechanism 2 in network terms → mechanism 3 declared unmeasured, outsourced to a citation.

**The transferable move:** *the hedge gradient is monotone.* Certainty declines strictly from sentence 1 to sentence 10 (demonstrated → likely → may also → we do not quantify). The paragraph never re-escalates. This is the single cheapest way to make a dense paragraph readable.

**Paragraph 2 (assumptions about vein contacts) — 4 sentences. A limitation paragraph.**

| # | Function |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Genre-normalising frame + naming.** States that analyses of this class always rest on under-determined morphological assumptions, then names theirs specifically. |
| 2 | **Operational statement of the assumption + what was not tested + direction of the possible error.** All three in one sentence: they treated every vein contact as a functional loop; no anatomical cross-sections were done; some contacts may not reroute fluid. |
| 3 | **Literature triangulation showing the ambiguity is general and quantified elsewhere.** Two independent living-plant systems cited, one with an actual frequency. |
| 4 | **Forward pointer naming the decisive measurement, with precedent.** Anatomy plus a specific dye-flow experiment, and a citation to someone who already did it on other taxa. |

**Skeleton:** normalise → name the assumption operationally → say what was not tested and which way it could bias → show with literature that the ambiguity is real and quantified elsewhere → name the decisive future measurement and cite a precedent for it.

**Paragraph 4 ("to what extent do our results inform…") — 7 sentences. A scope-boundary paragraph.**

| # | Function |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Rhetorical question naming the big question the reader wants answered.** |
| 2 | **Precise, scope-tagged restatement of what was actually shown** — with an explicit "the leaves we analysed here" limiter and both attack levels named. |
| 3 | **Concession: plausibility ≠ demonstration**, plus the study design that would settle it (longer time series, denser sampling). |
| 4 | **Design extension** (covariates). |
| 5 | **Rival hypothesis named explicitly** (physiological performance) with its own citations, and an admission that their result cannot adjudicate between the rivals. |
| 6 | **A published null result imported that pressures their own interpretation**, plus identification of the bridging analysis (a fluid-flow model) that would connect topology to physiology. |
| 7 | **Neutral/non-adaptive alternative named** — the hypothesis their design structurally cannot exclude. |

**Skeleton:** pose the reader's question → restate what was shown, scope-tagged → concede plausibility ≠ demonstration and name the missing design → enumerate rivals including a neutral one → import contrary/null literature → name the bridging analysis that would cross the level boundary.

**Paragraph 5 (closing) — 4 sentences. A significance-deflation paragraph.**

| # | Function |
|---|---|
| 1 | **External fact that undercuts the significance of their own finding.** The trait they showed to be advantageous was ecologically marginal for tens of millions of years. |
| 2 | **Explicit inference from it:** the advantage did not pay off on the timescale of its origin. |
| 3 | **Confound acknowledged:** the later success of reticulate-veined plants is attributable to a bundle of other innovations, cited. |
| 4 | **Final calibrated claim:** retains the mechanistic finding (robustness, "as demonstrated here", figure-anchored), discards the adaptive narrative, and offers a deliberately modest reframing ("an alternative design solution"). |

**Skeleton:** external fact that deflates → the deflating inference → confound acknowledged → keep the mechanism, drop the story, offer the modest frame.

## PART 2 — THE PATTERN LIBRARY

Five of your starting hypotheses survive with amendments; nine are added. Three general amendments apply to A–E and are folded into P1–P5 below:

- **Citations are not a beat.** In every paper I read, standalone citation sentences occur only when a citation *does work*: converting a number into another currency (Price s4), explaining an anomalous number of one's own (Price para 3 s4), or supplying a measurement the authors lack (Mander para 3 s10). Elsewhere, agreement with literature is fused into the claim sentence. "→ literature →" as a fixed slot is the main cause of result-citation-result-citation prose.
- **Hedge monotonicity.** Confidence must decrease across the paragraph and never re-escalate.
- **Every paragraph ends at its own boundary.** The last sentence states the implication *or* the forbidden inference — never a new result.

---

### P1 — Anchor Result *(revises Pattern A)*

**When:** the paragraph's job is to establish one number as the paper's reference point.

**Skeleton:** (1) claim, scope-tagged; (2) magnitude with sample and unanimity; (3) comparison to an internal reference, not an external one; (4) network mechanism; (5) *only if it does work*, literature; (6) implication in its own currency; (7) one-clause limit.

**Worked example:**
> Across the 100 leaves analysed, site percolation on the reconstructed venation graphs gave a median critical occupancy of p_c = 0.200 under random vein loss. The value was remarkably invariant: no leaf in the sample departed from the ordering seen under targeted removal, and all 100 of 100 showed a lower threshold when high-degree nodes were removed first. Against the synthetic reference lattice, whose random threshold is 0.271, real venation networks therefore fragment after losing a *smaller* fraction of their vein segments. The gap is a connectivity effect: real leaf graphs carry a large population of low-degree, terminal and near-terminal segments that contribute little redundancy, so the giant component depends on a thinner set of load-bearing junctions than a regular lattice does. Read as a robustness statement, this means a leaf's venation network tolerates roughly one-fifth of its vein segments being lost at random before global connectivity fails — a topological statement about the graph, not a prediction of the point at which the leaf wilts.

**Failure mode:** the paragraph becomes a caption. Symptom — three or more numbers in the first two sentences, none of them compared to anything. Second failure: comparing to an *external* literature value in sentence 3, which forces you to defend two methodologies at once before you have established your own.

**Length:** 6–8 sentences, 140–200 words.

---

### P2 — Predictor *(revises Pattern B)*

**When:** an architectural variable is claimed to explain variation in an outcome.

**Amendment:** strong writing states **what the statistic rules out**, not merely its value. The partial correlation is not decoration — it is the sentence that names the rival it eliminates.

**Skeleton:** (1) relationship + coefficient; (2) what survives control, and *for what* it was controlled; (3) the rival that this eliminates, named; (4) how much is still unexplained; (5) mechanism; (6) literature only if it converts or reconciles; (7) implication; (8) the causal claim explicitly not made.

**Worked example:**
> Percolation threshold tracked network connectivity closely across the empirical sample (Spearman ρ = 0.733 with mean degree; ρ = 0.727 with loop fraction). Because mean degree and loop fraction are themselves strongly related in real leaves, the relevant test is the partial: controlling for the remaining architectural descriptors, the connectivity–threshold association was undiminished at ρ = 0.736. This rules out the reading that connectivity is standing proxy for overall vein density or for leaf size within this sample. It does not, however, close the account: the full architectural model explained only R² = 0.507 of the variance in p_c, so roughly half the between-leaf variation lies outside the descriptors measured here. Mechanistically the association is what percolation theory anticipates — mean degree sets the expected number of surviving neighbours per retained node, and it is that quantity, not the count of veins, that determines whether a spanning cluster persists. Because these are observational relationships across 100 leaves that differ in many correlated ways at once, we treat them as identifying candidate predictors and defer the causal claim to the synthetic experiment described below.

**Failure mode:** reporting ρ and the partial ρ as two numbers rather than as a claim and its defence. Symptom — the reader cannot say which alternative explanation the partial was computed to kill. Second failure: quoting R² as a success when it is the paragraph's main concession.

**Length:** 6–9 sentences, 160–230 words.

### P3 — Attack-Mode Comparison *(revises Pattern C)*

**When:** two or more removal strategies are contrasted.

**Amendment from Del Ferraro:** the **gap between modes is the result**; neither threshold alone is. And each mode must be given an explicit adversary-realism status, because a reader will otherwise assume betweenness-targeting is something that happens in nature.

**Skeleton:** (1) modes and thresholds side by side; (2) the gap named as the quantity of interest, with its magnitude; (3) why the global metric outperforms the local one; (4) what the gap size says about the architecture; (5) adversary realism for each mode, stated separately; (6) implication; (7) the ecological claim not made.

**Worked example:**
> On the synthetic reference network the three removal strategies separated cleanly: p_c = 0.271 under random loss, 0.2023 under degree-targeted loss, and 0.1889 under betweenness-targeted loss. The informative quantity is the ordering and the spacing — targeting by a *global* flow property costs the network more than targeting by a *local* one, even though degree is the cruder and cheaper statistic. High-degree nodes are locally rich but frequently interchangeable, whereas high-betweenness nodes sit on a disproportionate share of shortest paths between otherwise weakly linked regions, so their removal disconnects rather than merely thins. In the empirical leaves the same ordering held without exception: median p_c fell from 0.200 under random loss to 0.169 under degree-targeting, a 15.7% reduction, in 100 of 100 networks. The three modes differ sharply in biological realism and should not be read as three scenarios: random loss is a reasonable idealisation of cavitation and of distributed mechanical damage; degree-targeted loss approximates damage concentrated at vein junctions; betweenness-targeted loss has no known biological agent and is included as an upper bound on achievable damage per unit removed. The comparison therefore bounds the vulnerability of venation architecture; it does not describe any organism's attack strategy.

**Failure mode:** the reader infers that a parasite, pathogen or herbivore performs targeted attack. Prevented only by an explicit realism sentence per mode. Second failure: giving all three thresholds equal narrative weight when only the ordering is defensible.

**Length:** 6–8 sentences, 170–220 words.

---

### P4 — Surprise *(revises Pattern D)*

**When:** a result contradicts a stated prior expectation.

**Amendment:** a surprise paragraph is only publishable if it (a) sources the expectation to something specific, and (b) states the conditions under which the surprise would disappear. Mander and Johnson both dissolve surprises by identifying which component is rate-limiting instead.

**Skeleton:** (1) the expectation, with its source; (2) the observed contradiction, with the number; (3) the mechanism that dissolves the surprise; (4) literature that either predicted or contradicts it; (5) the conditions under which the finding would flip; (6) what would make the surprise an artifact instead; (7) the cautious retained claim.

**Worked example:**
> Fractal dimension is among the most frequently reported descriptors of venation architecture, and its moderate association with the percolation threshold in our sample (ρ = 0.496; partial ρ = 0.503 after controlling for the other descriptors) initially suggested a second, geometry-side determinant alongside connectivity. The synthetic experiment does not support that reading. When mean degree and fractal dimension were varied independently on a controlled grid, the threshold followed connectivity almost deterministically (ρ = 0.991) and the standardised effect of mean degree exceeded that of fractal dimension by roughly 14 to 1. The most economical explanation is that fractal dimension and mean degree covary in real leaves, so that the empirical association of D with p_c is largely a covariate signature rather than an independent geometric mechanism — a possibility that only a design in which the two are decoupled can detect. This does not make D uninformative: it remains a compact descriptor of space-filling and would recover explanatory power in any sample where connectivity varies little. It does mean that reporting D as an architectural predictor of percolation robustness, without a decoupled control, risks attributing to geometry an effect that belongs to connectivity.

**Failure mode:** the surprise is announced and then dissolved so thoroughly that the reader cannot tell whether anything was found. Fix: sentence 5 must return partial credit to the demoted variable. Second failure: hedging the surprise itself ("we were somewhat surprised") rather than the explanation.

**Length:** 6–8 sentences, 170–220 words.

---

### P5 — Convergence *(revises Pattern E)*

**When:** two or more **independent** lines point the same way.

**Amendment — this is the pattern most often faked.** If two results share a data source, a metric, or a solver, they are repetition, not convergence. State the independence explicitly or do not claim it.

**Skeleton:** (1) name the lines and assert their independence, with the reason they are independent; (2) the invariant they share; (3) the network mechanism that generates the invariant; (4) literature triangulation; (5) what the convergence licenses, in one sentence; (6) what it does not license.

**Worked example:**
> Three lines of evidence, differing in their data source and in what they hold fixed, converge on connectivity as the controlling architectural variable. In the observational sample, mean degree was the strongest correlate of the threshold (ρ = 0.733; partial ρ = 0.736). In the synthetic grid — which shares no data with the empirical set and by construction decouples connectivity from fractal dimension — the association strengthened to ρ = 0.991 with R² = 0.887. And in the reticulation intervention, raising mean degree to 2.811 on the real graphs themselves moved the median threshold from 0.201 to 0.215, improving 93 of 105 leaves and worsening none. The invariant across all three is that the threshold responds to the expected number of surviving neighbours per retained node, and to that quantity whether it is observed, manufactured, or added. This licenses the claim that connectivity is causally upstream of percolation robustness in planar venation graphs. It does not license the claim that connectivity is the dominant determinant of robustness in real leaves under real damage, because all three lines share the same failure criterion — loss of the giant component — and none of them measures water.

**Failure mode:** three results that are the same result computed three ways. The diagnostic question: could any one of the three have come out the other way while the other two held? If not, it is repetition.

**Length:** 6–8 sentences, 180–230 words.

### P6 — Informative Null *(new; modelled on Johnson et al.'s no-advantage paragraph and Price's non-significant-difference paragraph)*

**When:** a mechanism you built, calibrated and expected to matter produced no effect.

**Skeleton:** (1) the null stated flatly, with the *full* range sampled, so the reader knows it is not an undersampled null; (2) the magnitude of the perturbation that failed to produce the effect — this is what makes the null strong; (3) the component that is rate-limiting *instead*; (4) the physics that makes the null close to inevitable; (5) the flip conditions — what parameter change would produce a positive; (6) literature with the same or opposite sign; (7) the reframing of what the system is actually limited by; (8) the null's limit of generality.

**Worked example:**
> The calibrated haustorial sink did not cavitate the host network under any condition we imposed. Across all 31 imposed potential gradients spanning 0 to 3.0 MPa, zero vessels embolised, despite the sink diverting approximately 20% of total network flow — a diversion large enough that a purely flux-based argument would predict substantial hydraulic stress. The null arises because the failure criterion is a potential, not a flux: the most negative host water potential reached anywhere in the network was −1.02 MPa, roughly half the −2.0 MPa embolism threshold, so the network never approached the condition under which vessels fail. The reason a large flux diversion produces such a modest potential excursion is the resistance structure of the network itself: with conductance scaling as the fourth power of conduit radius, flow is overwhelmingly carried by the widest elements, and the sink draws through those low-resistance paths without generating the tension that failure requires. The null is therefore conditional and its flip conditions are specific: a sink placed on high-resistance minor venation, a substantially larger diversion fraction, a lower embolism threshold, or a host already pre-tensioned by soil drying would each move the minimum potential toward the failure criterion. What the result reframes is the locus of parasite-induced damage — under this parameterisation the host is limited by carbon and water *removal*, not by vascular failure, and pre-symptomatic collapse, if it occurs, is unlikely to be a cavitation cascade. This conclusion is bounded by the single sink geometry and the single calibration tested here, and does not generalise to multi-haustorial infections or to hosts near their own hydraulic limits.

**Failure mode (three, all common):** (i) the null is stated apologetically, as a failed experiment, rather than as a constraint on the mechanism space; (ii) no flip conditions are given, so the reader cannot judge whether the null is informative or merely undersampled; (iii) the null is over-generalised into a claim about parasitism in general. The single most important sentence is (3) — naming what *is* rate-limiting.

**Length:** 7–9 sentences, 200–260 words. Nulls need more space than positives, not less.

### P7 — Failed Validation *(new; modelled on Johnson et al.'s self-critique paragraph)*

**When:** a validation test the paper set for itself did not pass.

**Non-negotiable structural requirement:** the paragraph must **partition the paper's own claims by dependency on the failed test.** That partition is what makes a failed validation publishable rather than fatal, and no other pattern supplies it.

**Skeleton:** (1) the test, the prediction, and the statistic including the non-significant p and n; (2) plain statement that it failed — no euphemism, no "trend"; (3) candidate causes, ordered from *our model is wrong* to *the comparison is mis-specified*; (4) the evidence that discriminates among them, or an explicit statement that none does; (5) which claims in this paper depend on the test; (6) which do not, and why; (7) the measurement that would settle it.

**Worked example:**
> We tested whether network-integrated conductance computed from the reconstructed graphs predicts independently measured whole-leaf hydraulic conductance across the 93 species for which both were available. It does not: the rank correlation was ρ = 0.187 (p = 0.073), which we report as a failure of the test rather than as a marginal result, since at this sample size the interval is compatible with no association at all. Three explanations are consistent with this outcome and we cannot presently separate them. The first is that the Hagen–Poiseuille network solve is a poor model of leaf conductance — plausible, since conductance scales with the fourth power of an inferred conduit radius that we do not measure directly. The second is that the comparison is mis-specified at the level of the quantity itself: measured whole-leaf conductance includes the outside-xylem pathway through bundle sheath and mesophyll, which our graph does not represent at all, so the two quantities need not correlate even if the xylem model is correct. The third is that the source imagery consists of crop windows rather than whole blades, so the reconstructed graph is a sample of each leaf's venation rather than its complete conducting network, and integrated conductance is exactly the kind of extensive quantity such truncation destroys. All of the paper's percolation results are independent of this test — they depend on the graph's topology, not on the absolute magnitude of its conductances — whereas the haustorial sink simulation, which is posed in units of water potential, does depend on the conductance model being approximately right, and its null result should be read with that dependency in view. A paired dataset of whole-blade venation images with matched K_leaf measurements on the same individuals would discriminate among the three explanations; the present data cannot.

**Failure mode:** treating p = 0.073 as weak support. Second failure: burying the test among validations that passed, which prevents the dependency partition from being visible. Third: proposing a cause without saying what would distinguish it from the others.

**Length:** 8–11 sentences, 240–320 words. This is the longest paragraph in the Discussion and should be.

### P8 — Withdrawn Claim *(new; no exemplar in the papers I read — none of them reported an uninterpretable analysis, which is itself informative about how rare this move is)*

**When:** an analysis was run, is reported for completeness, and settles nothing.

**Skeleton:** (1) what was computed and why it was expected to be informative; (2) the result, briefly; (3) the specific reason it is uninterpretable — a named confound, insufficient range, or design flaw, not vague uncertainty; (4) the statement that it is reported so that a later reader does not re-run it and misread it; (5) the design that would make it interpretable; (6) an explicit sentence forbidding the inference.

**Worked example:**
> We had intended to use a finite-size scaling analysis to test whether the thresholds reported here represent a genuine critical point or a size-dependent artifact of the reconstructed graphs. We report the analysis but withdraw the claim we had hoped to base on it. The empirical networks do not span a sufficient or systematically controlled range of sizes for the scaling exponent to be identified: network size in this sample covaries with species, with imaging window, and with extraction quality, so any apparent size dependence is confounded with all three. We include the result rather than omitting it because the analysis is a natural one for a reader to attempt on the same data, and it should be known in advance that this dataset cannot support it. Settling the question requires a size series constructed within single species, or synthetic networks sampled across decades of size at fixed architecture. Until then, no inference about the sharpness, universality class, or size-independence of the thresholds reported here should be drawn from this analysis in either direction.

**Failure mode:** reporting the withdrawn analysis at a length proportional to the work it took rather than to the information it carries. Second failure: hedging so softly that a reader extracts a weak positive from it. Sentence (6) must be a prohibition, not a caution.

**Length:** 5–7 sentences, 130–180 words. **Never longer than the paragraph reporting a result it does not support.**

### P9 — Scope Boundary *(new; modelled directly on Mander & Williams' "to what extent" paragraph)*

**When:** the paper operates at one level of description and readers will import conclusions at another. For you: topology vs physiology, and the paper's own statement that these are decoupled lenses.

**Skeleton:** (1) pose the question the reader will ask, directly; (2) restate precisely what the model represents and in what units; (3) name the quantity the model does *not* contain; (4) give the design reason the two levels are decoupled here; (5) cite where the bridge exists in the literature; (6) name the bridging analysis; (7) state the interpretation the boundary forbids.

**Worked example:**
> Does a leaf with a lower percolation threshold suffer more from parasitic attack? This study cannot answer that, and the reason is structural rather than a matter of statistical power. The percolation analysis is posed entirely in topological terms: its state variable is the fraction of vein segments retained, and its failure criterion is the loss of a spanning connected component. The hydraulic analysis is posed entirely in physical terms: its state variable is water potential in MPa, and its failure criterion is embolism at −2.0 MPa. The two share a graph but not a currency, and nothing in this study converts one into the other — which is precisely why the two results can be, and are, mutually silent: a network whose threshold falls from 0.200 to 0.169 under targeting is not thereby closer to the −1.02 MPa minimum potential the sink actually produced. Bridging them requires an analysis this paper does not perform: a coupled model in which vein removal alters the resistance network, the altered network alters the potential field, and the potential field determines further removal, so that topological fragmentation and hydraulic failure become one process rather than two descriptions. Until such a coupling exists, p_c should be read as a measure of architectural redundancy and not as a predictor of wilting, of leaf water status, or of host mortality under parasitism.

**Failure mode:** the boundary is stated as a limitation and then quietly crossed two paragraphs later. Once you write this paragraph, every subsequent claim must respect it — including in the Abstract.

**Length:** 6–8 sentences, 180–230 words.

### P10 — Novelty Positioning *(new; modelled on Ronellenfitsch et al. paragraph 1)*

**When:** stating what is new, in a field where close prior art exists.

**Skeleton:** (1) name the contribution class; (2) name prior art precisely and say what it does *well*; (3) name the exact operational defect — the thing it cannot do, in mechanical terms, not "limited"; (4) state the increment as an added dimension or a first joint measurement; (5) the concrete capability the increment delivers; (6) concede the increment's smallness where true; (7) the priority claim not made.

**Worked example:**
> The contribution here is the joint treatment of venation as a percolation substrate and as a hydraulic resistor network on the same reconstructed graphs. Both components have strong precedents. Simulated damage to real and fossil venation networks has established that loopy architectures resist fragmentation better than branching ones, and topological decompositions of leaf venation have shown that loop-nesting structure carries phenotypic information that density-based descriptors do not. What has not been available is a calibrated threshold: prior damage simulations report robustness as a curve or a contrast between architectural classes, not as a critical occupancy fraction estimated per network and regressed against measured architecture. The increment is that estimate and what it enables — a per-leaf p_c that can be compared across 100 species, decomposed into architectural predictors, tested causally on a decoupled synthetic grid, and moved by a defined intervention. The increment is genuinely narrow: it adds a scalar and a causal warrant to an existing qualitative result, and the intervention it supports shifts the median threshold by 0.014, or by 0.0069 on the artifact-free subset. We therefore claim neither the first percolation treatment of venation networks nor the first demonstration that reticulation confers robustness, both of which are established.

**Failure mode:** priority language ("for the first time", "novel framework") that a reviewer familiar with the adjacent literature can falsify in one sentence — the fastest route to a hostile review. Second failure: critiquing prior art vaguely; sentence (3) must name a *mechanical* defect.

**Length:** 6–8 sentences, 170–220 words.

### P11 — Assumption Audit *(new; modelled on Price & Weitz paragraph 6)*

**When:** consolidating modelling assumptions. One paragraph, not a scattered set of apologies.

**Skeleton:** (1) enumerate assumptions ordered from safest to riskiest; (2) defend the safe ones by citation in one sentence each; (3) declare the riskiest unverified and say *why* it is unverified; (4) theoretical plausibility argument, with its scope condition; (5) adjacent-system evidence; (6) explicit refusal to bank the transfer.

**Worked example:**
> The results rest on four assumptions of decreasing security. That xylem conductance follows Hagen–Poiseuille scaling is well established and is not at issue. That vein calibre in the source imagery is proportional to conduit calibre is standard but not directly verified here, and the fourth-power dependence makes any departure from proportionality disproportionately consequential — this is the assumption on which the failed K_leaf validation bears most directly. That vein loss is appropriately modelled as *site* rather than *bond* removal is a modelling choice: site percolation removes junctions and all segments incident to them, which represents damage localised at vein intersections, whereas cavitation propagating along a conduit is better represented as bond removal, and the two do not share a threshold. Finally, treating the reconstructed graph as the complete conducting network is not correct for this dataset, because the source images are crop windows rather than whole blades and contain no petiole, so that no root or entry node is enforced and the spanning criterion is defined on an interior fragment. We report the site-percolation results because they are the ones we computed and calibrated, but the site-versus-bond choice should be understood as an unresolved specification rather than a settled one, and a bond-percolation replicate on the same graphs would be the first thing we would want from an extension of this work.

**Failure mode:** listing assumptions without ranking them, which reads as either exhaustive honesty or evasion depending on the reviewer's mood, and gives no one a target. The ordering *is* the argument.

**Length:** 6–9 sentences, 200–260 words.

### P12 — Magnitude Explanation *(new; modelled on Price & Weitz's 0.51% paragraph)*

**When:** a number is surprisingly small or surprisingly large and the reader will not otherwise believe it.

**Skeleton:** (1) the number; (2) the governing law or scaling that sets its size; (3) the law's consequence in plain language; (4) derive the number's order of magnitude from the law; (5) situate it in the whole-system budget; (6) the range over which the derivation holds.

**Worked example:**
> Raising mean degree to 2.811 by adding reticulation moved the median threshold from 0.201 to 0.215 — a gain of 0.014, or under 7% of the baseline. The modest size follows from where percolation thresholds live: the threshold is governed by the expected number of surviving neighbours per retained node, a quantity that responds to mean degree roughly reciprocally rather than proportionally, so a fractional increase in connectivity buys a smaller fractional decrease in the fraction that must be removed. The added connections are also, necessarily, the cheapest ones — short links between already-adjacent minor veins — and these contribute a single alternative path each rather than restructuring the network's backbone. For comparison, the entire architectural model explains R² = 0.507 of the between-leaf variance in p_c, so an intervention worth 0.014 is small relative to the natural spread it is trying to move. The derivation holds only within the connectivity range explored here; a network driven far above the planar reticulation limit would be a different object and the scaling above should not be extrapolated to it.

**Failure mode:** the number is defended rather than explained. If the paragraph reads as an apology for a small effect, it has failed; it should read as a derivation that predicts the smallness.

**Length:** 5–7 sentences, 150–200 words.

### P13 — Reconciling Disagreeing Estimates *(new; no exemplar in the papers I read — none reported two conflicting estimates of the same quantity. You must build this one from first principles, and it is the highest-risk paragraph in the paper.)*

**When:** two of your own analyses give different values for what a reader will assume is the same quantity.

**Skeleton:** (1) both estimates, with their numbers, side by side; (2) the discrepancy stated explicitly as a ratio — never let the reader compute it; (3) the categorical difference between them — which is an intervention and which is a prediction, which is within-sample and which is extrapolated; (4) the reason they need not agree, in mechanistic terms; (5) which one the paper's conclusions use, and why; (6) what would make them agree; (7) an explicit prohibition on splitting the difference.

**Worked example:**
> Two of our analyses give different answers for how much percolation robustness a given increase in connectivity should buy, and they differ by a factor of roughly 3.7. The response surface fitted to the empirical sample predicts one gain from raising mean degree to 2.811; directly performing that intervention on the real graphs produced a median gain of 0.014. These are not two measurements of one quantity. The response surface is an extrapolation from between-leaf variation — it asks what threshold a leaf with this connectivity would be expected to have, given how leaves that differ in connectivity also differ in everything else. The intervention asks what happens to *this* leaf when connectivity alone is changed, holding its remaining architecture fixed. Between-leaf variation and within-leaf perturbation are not interchangeable whenever the predictor covaries with unmeasured architecture, which is exactly the situation the fractal-dimension result documents, and a discrepancy in this direction — extrapolation exceeding intervention — is what that confounding predicts. Our conclusions therefore rest on the intervention, which is the causally interpretable of the two, and we report the response-surface prediction as a diagnostic of how much of the empirical association is carried by covariates rather than by connectivity itself. Reconciling them requires interventions spanning a wider connectivity range on the same graphs. The intermediate value is not an estimate of anything and should not be reported as one.

**Failure mode:** presenting the disagreement as a minor inconsistency, or worse, presenting only one of the two. If a reader can compute a discrepancy from your own tables that you did not name, the paper has lost the ability to control its interpretation. Sentence (2) must appear before any explanation.

**Length:** 7–10 sentences, 220–290 words.

### P14 — Artifact Triage / Subset Robustness *(new; nearest exemplar is Mander & Williams' vein-contact assumption paragraph, but that one is prospective — yours is retrospective and needs a decision)*

**When:** an effect attenuates on a data-quality subset.

**Skeleton:** (1) the whole-sample effect; (2) the clean-subset effect; (3) attenuation stated as a comparison, not two separate numbers; (4) whether the *sign* changed or only the magnitude — this distinction carries most of the weight; (5) the estimate of record, and the justification for choosing it; (6) what the attenuation implies about the discarded portion; (7) the data that would remove the need for triage.

**Worked example:**
> On the full sample the reticulation intervention was near-universal in direction: 93 of 105 leaves improved, none worsened, and the median threshold rose by 0.014. On the subset carrying the highest extraction-quality grade, the intervention improved only 62% of leaves and the median gain fell to 0.0069, roughly half. What did not change is the sign — no leaf in either sample was made more fragile by added reticulation — and this is the more robust of the two findings, since a direction that survives an aggressive quality filter is unlikely to be a reconstruction artifact. What did change is the magnitude and the unanimity, and the most economical explanation is that lower-grade reconstructions contain spurious breaks in genuine veins, which added reticulation repairs; the intervention therefore does double duty on those networks, partly adding real redundancy and partly undoing extraction error. We accordingly treat the grade-A estimate of 0.0069 as the estimate of record and the full-sample 0.014 as an upper bound, rather than averaging or reporting only the larger. The residual uncertainty is a property of the source imagery, not of the model, and would be resolved by re-running the intervention on venation reconstructions validated against the original images.

**Failure mode:** reporting the full-sample number in the Abstract and the subset number only in the Discussion. Second failure: declaring the subset "the true result" without arguing why the filter removes bias rather than merely removing data — sentence (6) is that argument.

**Length:** 6–8 sentences, 180–240 words.

## PART 3 — DENSITY RULES

What separates the dense paragraphs I read (Mander para 3, ten sentences with three ranked mechanisms; Price para 5, five sentences deriving a number from a scaling law) from crammed ones is not the number of facts. It is that every sentence consumes the previous sentence's output. Eleven operational rules:

**1. One paragraph, one falsifiable claim.** Multiple results are permitted in a paragraph only when they are *evidence for the same claim that could have come out otherwise*. Test: write the claim as a single sentence that could be wrong. If you need "and" to write it, you have two paragraphs.

**2. Every number does a different job.** If two numbers in a paragraph support the same inference, one is decoration. Move it to a table. In Price's 0.51% paragraph there is exactly one number in five sentences, and the other four sentences explain it.

**3. Citations go where they do work — never as a beat.** Three legitimate jobs, all observed in the papers I read: converting your number into another currency (Price s4); explaining an anomalous number of your own (Price para 3 s4); supplying a measurement you lack (Mander para 3 s10). Agreement with prior work is fused into the claim sentence, not given a sentence. The "→ literature →" slot in your starting hypotheses is the main mechanism by which result-citation-result-citation prose is generated; delete the slot and let citations land where they earn their place.

**4. Hedge monotonicity.** Confidence must decrease across the paragraph. Mander runs demonstrated → likely → may also → not quantified here, in that order, and never reverses. A paragraph that hedges in sentence 4 and then asserts confidently in sentence 7 reads as crammed even when it is short, because the reader must re-calibrate mid-paragraph.

**5. At most two currency changes.** Topology → hydraulics → ecology is two changes and is readable. Adding evolution or agronomy is a third and breaks the paragraph. Count the units the reader must hold: occupancy fraction, MPa, ρ, %, species — four is the ceiling.

**6. Limitations attach to the claim they limit.** Price pays for his extrapolation in the very next sentence. The exception is limitations of the *whole dataset* — the crop windows, the missing petioles, the extraction grade — which belong in one place because they limit everything and repeating them dilutes them.

**7. The deletion test.** If a sentence can be removed without breaking the chain from the sentence before it to the sentence after, it does not belong in this paragraph. It belongs in another paragraph, in a table, or nowhere.

**8. Topic-and-tail test for cramming.** Read only the first and last sentences. If they are not about the same object, the paragraph is two paragraphs wearing one coat. This is the fastest diagnostic and it catches most cases.

**9. The two-figure rule.** If a paragraph requires the reader to hold two figures in mind simultaneously *and they are different kinds of plot*, split it. Two panels of the same figure are fine; a threshold curve plus a scatter plot are not.

**10. Density comes from resolution, not from adjacency.** The high-information sentences in the papers I read are ones where a fact from the literature resolves a tension in the authors' own data (Price's veinlet confound explaining his own low meshedness). Two facts placed next to each other add; a fact that dissolves another multiplies. When you want a paragraph denser, look for a resolution, not for another sentence.

**11. State what survives.** When a paragraph floats competing mechanisms, close it with the claim that holds regardless of which is right (Del Ferraro's "regardless of the specific microcircuit"). This is what lets you be dense and non-committal at once without seeming evasive — and your Discussion needs it in at least three places.

## PART 4 — DO NOT COMBINE

**1. The fractal-dimension result must not share a paragraph with the connectivity result.** They carry opposite burdens of proof. The connectivity paragraph makes a positive causal claim licensed by the decoupled grid; the D paragraph makes a *demotion* — that a respectable empirical correlation (ρ = 0.496, partial 0.503) is largely a covariate signature, given the 14:1 standardised dominance when the two are decoupled. Presenting 0.727/0.733 and 0.496 in one paragraph invites the reader to rank them on a common scale as two predictors of differing strength, which is exactly the reading the synthetic experiment refutes. Separate paragraphs, with the D paragraph placed *after* the causal grid so that the demotion has its warrant already on the table.

**2. The haustorial sink must not share a paragraph with the percolation threshold.** The paper itself calls these decoupled lenses, and combining them performs the inference the null forbids: that the sink acts *through* the threshold. They do not share a state variable (retained fraction vs MPa), a failure criterion (giant-component loss vs embolism at −2.0 MPa), or a perturbation (removal vs flux diversion). Any sentence containing both 0.169 and −1.02 MPa asserts a relationship the paper has not established. The only paragraph permitted to hold both is the scope-boundary paragraph, whose explicit job is to say they do not connect.

**3. The synthetic threshold must not share a paragraph with the empirical threshold as a comparison of magnitudes.** Two reasons. First, 0.271 and 0.200 differ partly because of architecture and partly because of construction — the synthetic reference is a designed lattice at 10⁶ trials while other comparisons run at 10⁵ — so the difference is not a clean biological effect and putting them adjacent invites reading it as one. Second and more important, they answer different questions: the synthetic grid is a *causal instrument* (does connectivity drive p_c?), the empirical sample is an *observational description* (what is p_c in real leaves?). Use the synthetic paragraph as the causal warrant that licenses mechanistic language in the empirical paragraph, with an explicit forward or backward reference — not as a magnitude comparison.

**4. The enhancement gain must not share a paragraph with the response-surface prediction.** A 3.7-fold disagreement embedded in flowing prose reads either as an error or as concealment. It is itself a result — evidence about how much of the empirical association is carried by covariates — and needs the dedicated reconciliation paragraph (P13) that classifies one as intervention and the other as extrapolation. Additionally, do not combine the enhancement result, the grade-A triage, *and* the prediction disagreement in one paragraph: that is three unresolved tensions, and the reader will retain none of them. Three paragraphs: the intervention, the triage, the reconciliation.

**5. The failed K_leaf validation must not share a paragraph with any validation that passed.** A paragraph listing checks that passed and then one that did not converts the failure into a footnote, and — decisively — makes the dependency partition invisible. The reader must be able to see, in one place, which of the paper's claims survive ρ = 0.187 and which do not. That partition cannot be made inside a list.

**6. The withdrawn finite-size claim must not appear near the synthetic threshold values.** Any proximity of a withdrawn size-scaling analysis to 0.271 / 0.2023 / 0.1889 contaminates those values with a suspicion of size dependence that the withdrawal was not about. The withdrawn claim belongs late, alone, and short.

**7. The three-mode attack comparison must not be merged with the empirical two-mode result as a single ordering.** The empirical sample has random and degree-targeted arms only; there is no empirical betweenness arm at matched trial count. A paragraph that flows from 0.271/0.2023/0.1889 into 0.200/0.169 without marking the transition will leave the reader believing a betweenness threshold was measured on real leaves. Marking is sufficient here — these two *can* share a paragraph, unlike the pairs above, provided the sentence boundary between synthetic and empirical is explicit.

**What *may* safely be combined:** the loop-fraction correlations (+0.818 with the targeted threshold, −0.786 with the random-minus-targeted gap) belong together and only together, because they are two faces of one claim — loops buy robustness against random loss while narrowing the margin that targeting can exploit. Separating them would make each look like an isolated correlation.
