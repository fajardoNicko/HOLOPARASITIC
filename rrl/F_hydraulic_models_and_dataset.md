## THE MATOS DATASET — WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS

**Correct citation structure (the paper currently gets this wrong).** There are two 2024 Matos papers on the same 122 leaves and they must be cited separately:
- **Data paper:** Matos et al. (2024), *Ecology*, 105(12), e4436, DOI 10.1002/ecy.4436 — this is the article Dryad registers as the deposit's primary publication.
- **Analysis paper:** Matos et al. (2024), *New Phytologist*, 244(2), 407–425, DOI 10.1111/nph.20037, "Leaf venation network architecture coordinates functional trade-offs across vein spatial scales."
The shared context fuses these — it attaches the *New Phytologist* volume/pages/DOI to the *Ecology* dataset title. Fix before submission; a reviewer familiar with this group will notice immediately.

**Measurement protocol.** Sampled Feb–Sept 2021 from the UC Botanical Garden living collections. 31 functional plus several architectural traits across six axes. Maximum leaf hydraulic conductance by the **evaporative flux method** on fully rehydrated mature leaves; vulnerability curves (P50, P88) by EFM; gas exchange by LI-COR 6800; mechanics by three-point bending; chemistry by total phenols; venation from chemically cleared, stained, slide-mounted leaves.

**Extraction method.** LeafVeinCNN (Fricker, Blonder & Xu; v2.14.00, Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.8272938, CC-BY-4.0), an ensemble of six independently trained CNNs (Xu et al. 2021), precision-recall harmonic mean 94.5% ± 6%, validated for widths, angles and connectivity. v2.14 adds monocot and branching-only handling by extrapolating peripheral free-ending veins to the lamina margin — explicitly flagged as **beta**. Downstream analysis is hierarchical loop decomposition.

**Graph representation.** Segment-level and node-level tables. Segment fields include `Width` ("average width of the vein segment, including any overlap region in mm"), `Width_min`, length, and the two resistance columns. Node fields include `node_D0/D1/D2`, "diameter of the largest/middle/smallest vein incident at the node in mm" — i.e. the dataset natively supplies a **junction-level radius hierarchy**, which is exactly the quantity Murray's Law is used to estimate. **This is an actionable finding: the paper may be inferring radii the deposit already ships.** Scaling to physical units is via `image_resolution`, "image resolution in millimeters per pixel".

**Species/phylogenetic coverage.** 122 species, 112 families, 56 orders — ferns, basal angiosperms, monocots, basal eudicots, asterids, rosids; all continents except Antarctica; diverse growth forms. Unusually broad for a venation dataset.

**The resistance column's provenance — verbatim from the deposit's data dictionary:**
- `Resistance_4` = **"predicted resistance to flow based on Poiseuille flow i.e. l/r4"**, units mm⁻³
- `Resistance_2` = **"predicted resistance to flow based on the vein area i.e. l/r2"**, units mm⁻¹, glossed as "effectively treats the resistance as scaling with the number of equal sized vessels in a bundle"

Both are **purely geometric** — length and segmented vein width. No viscosity term, no conduit count, no pit correction. Note that `Resistance_4` is `l/r⁴`, not the full Hagen-Poiseuille `8µl/(πr⁴)`; it is a relative resistance up to the constant `8µ/π`.

**Quality grades — six tiers, not four.** A = excellent, all veins correct; B = medium, >80% of veins correct; C = medium, fake minor veins segmented; D = medium, leaf damage <25% of total leaf area; E = poor, <80% segmented; F = poor, damage >25%. The scale is **not ordinal** — B, C and D encode under-segmentation, over-segmentation and physical damage respectively, which have opposite effects on a percolation threshold (C inflates connectivity, B and E deflate it).

**Stated caveats and cross-species comparison.** The deposit acknowledges that CNN segmentation completeness and artifact rate vary by sample condition, encoded in the grade. The companion *New Phytologist* paper's own headline conclusion — architecture predicts support and damage resistance strongly but **efficiency and resilience weakly** — is itself the most important cross-species caveat available, and it directly anticipates this paper's weak K_leaf result.

## THE VALIDATION QUESTION

**Answer: yes, the concern is correct, and it is documented in the deposit's own data dictionary.**

The source column `Resistance_4` is defined verbatim as *"predicted resistance to flow based on Poiseuille flow i.e. l/r4"*. That is the same convention the paper's solver implements. Reproducing it to a worst-case per-leaf CV of 1.12e-07 therefore demonstrates that the paper **correctly re-implemented a shared geometric formula on the same input geometry** — a reproducibility and numerical-precision result. It is not evidence that l/r⁴ is the right physics for a leaf vein, because both sides of the comparison assume it. The wrong-exponent control (0.953) likewise only confirms the exponent is 4, not that 4 is physically correct.

Two further points that sharpen this:
1. The agreement is reported as the CV of a model-to-source **ratio**, which is exactly the signature expected when two implementations of the same kernel differ only by a constant prefactor (here, `8µ/π`, which the source column omits). This is fully consistent with, and only with, correct re-implementation.
2. `r` on both sides is derived from **segmented vein-segment width** — the radius of a whole vein bundle, which contains many conduits plus non-conducting parenchyma and fibres, not a xylem conduit lumen. The deposit's authors evidently regarded this as an open question themselves: they shipped `Resistance_2` (l/r²) alongside, explicitly because it "treats the resistance as scaling with the number of equal sized vessels in a bundle." **The paper chose one of two conventions the data providers deliberately offered as alternatives, and it must justify that choice rather than present the match as validation.**

**What the result does legitimately establish.** It is still worth reporting, at 5,668,467 veins across 119 leaves: it verifies the geometry parser, the unit conversion via `image_resolution`, the length and width extraction, the exponent, and the numerical conditioning of the Kirchhoff solve at scale, with no silent indexing, scaling or unit errors. That is a real and non-trivial software-correctness claim. It is simply not a physical one.

**Exact defensible phrasing the paper should use.** Replace any "validated against" language with:

> "The source deposit supplies a per-segment resistance column defined as `l/r⁴`, i.e. a purely geometric Hagen-Poiseuille relative resistance computed from segmented vein length and width (Matos et al., 2024, Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.1g1jwsv36). Our solver reproduces this column across 119 leaves and 5,668,467 vein segments to a worst-case per-leaf coefficient of variation of the resistance ratio of 1.12 × 10⁻⁷ (tolerance 1 × 10⁻⁶), whereas a control using an incorrect radius exponent reproduces it at only 0.953. **Because our conductance model adopts the same l/r⁴ convention as the source column, this agreement is a verification of correct re-implementation — of geometry parsing, unit conversion, and numerical conditioning at scale — and not an independent test of the physical validity of lumen-only Poiseuille scaling in leaf veins.** We report it as a software-correctness check. Independent physical validity is addressed separately against measured leaf hydraulic conductance (below), where agreement is weak, as expected for a xylem-only model."

And add, in Methods, the choice justification the deposit forces:

> "The deposit also supplies an alternative resistance convention, `l/r²`, which treats a vein as a bundle of equal-sized conduits whose number scales with cross-sectional area. We adopt `l/r⁴` because [state the reason], and report the sensitivity of p_c to this choice in [Supplementary]."

That sensitivity analysis is, in my judgement, the highest-value single addition available to this paper: `Resistance_2` is already in the data, requires no new modelling, and converts the most obvious reviewer objection into a robustness result. If p_c is stable across the r² and r⁴ conventions, the topological claim survives the radius-inference vulnerability entirely.

## COUNTEREVIDENCE AND LIMITS

**1. Pit/end-wall resistance omitted by the lumen-only model: ~54%.** Sperry, Hacke & Wheeler (2005) measured the end-wall fraction of total conduit resistivity across seven anatomically diverse taxa (vessel-bearing fern, tracheid gymnosperm, angiosperms with and without vessels) and found it approximately constant at **0.54 ± 0.07**. Secondary literature places the pit-membrane contribution in the range **30–80%**, with review consensus that pits account for **over 50%** of xylem resistance on average. The paper's r⁴/L conductance omits all of it. *Mitigation the paper can legitimately claim:* the fraction is approximately **constant** across taxa, so a multiplicative bias largely cancels in a **relative** quantity like p_c. Say that explicitly — it is the honest defence and it is stronger than silence.

**2. Outside-xylem share of leaf hydraulic resistance: ~26–39% typically, 11–74% across species, and 75–100% of the dehydration-driven decline.** Sack, Streeter & Holbrook (2004): 36% (sugar maple) and 26% (red oak) outside the venation. Sack, Tyree & Holbrook (2005): mean 39% outside-xylem across 10 rainforest species, range **11–74%**. Scoffoni et al. (2011): K_ox and K_x "of similar magnitude on average". Scoffoni et al. (2017): outside-xylem vulnerability explains **75% to 100% of K_leaf decline** through turgor loss point, with X-ray microCT showing only minimal xylem embolism at that point. Buckley et al. (2015, MOFLO, 14 species): the outside-xylem pathway has its own anatomy-dependent physics, with vapour transport contributing up to two-thirds of K_ox. **Consequence:** the paper's ρ = 0.187, p = 0.073 is the *predicted* outcome, not a failure — a xylem-only model omits a component that is 11–74% of the static resistance and varies independently across species. But Scoffoni et al. (2017) is also a genuine threat to the paper's mechanism, not merely to its validation, and the Discussion must confront it rather than cite it only as an excuse.

**3. Murray's Law fails systematically in major veins.** Price, Knox & Brodribb (2013), 349 species and >1.5 M vein segments: median exponent 2.96 (Murray predicts 3.0) — but a monotone shift by branch order, with large veins moving toward **area-preserving branching (exponent ≈ 2)**. McCulloh, Sperry & Adler (2003): plant conduits obey Murray's law *only where they are not also mechanical supports*. Major veins are load-bearing. **This is the worst-case combination for the paper**: Murray-inferred radii are least accurate exactly in major veins, where the r⁴ weighting makes each radius error most consequential. Bound this explicitly, and prefer the deposit's native `node_D0/D1/D2` junction diameters over inference wherever they exist.

**4. Venation topology predicts measured leaf hydraulics weakly — from the source study itself.** Matos et al. (2024, *New Phytologist* 244:407–425), same 122 leaves: architecture "strongly predicts leaf support and damage resistance axes but weakly predicts efficiency and resilience axes." Blonder et al. (2020, 260 species) reports the same asymmetry. Scoffoni et al. (2011) declines to quantify independent venation explanation of K_leaf variation at all. **Silver lining, and the paper should take it:** the axes venation *does* predict well are **support and damage resistance** — which is what a percolation threshold measures. The paper's own validation pattern (fails on efficiency/K_leaf, claims relevance to structural collapse) matches the literature's pattern exactly, and that congruence is a positive argument the paper is not currently making.

**5. Crop windows — the caveat is real but its provenance needs correcting.** Xu et al. (2021) state the CNN ensemble segments "larger leaf regions (c. 100 mm²)", and the sibling Bornean deposit (Blonder et al. 2019) used ~1 × 1 cm regions of interest with primary veins excluded. **However**, the Berkeley-lineage imaging protocol (Niewiadomski et al. 2025) is explicitly for **"whole leaf venation networks"**, with throughput given as "5–10 large whole leaves or 30–40 small leaves or leaf segments per week", and the deposit's quality grades D and F are defined against **"total leaf area"** — wording that only makes sense for whole blades. The deposit therefore appears to contain a **mixture** of whole leaves and leaf segments. The paper should verify the crop status of its own 100/120 files directly (compare each network's bounding box against the leaf mask and the `image_resolution` scale) rather than asserting a blanket crop-window claim that the protocol paper contradicts. If they are crops from mid-lamina with primaries excluded, the loss of the major-vein hierarchy is a first-order threat to a percolation result, because Sack et al. (2008) shows damage tolerance is governed precisely by primary/major-vein redundancy.

**6. The quality grade is non-ordinal, and grade C inflates connectivity.** C = "fake minor veins segmented" adds spurious edges; B and E = incomplete segmentation removes real edges. These bias p_c in **opposite** directions. Filtering by "grade ≥ D" is therefore not a valid monotone quality filter. The paper should report p_c stratified by grade, or at minimum confirm p_c is stable between grade-A leaves and the rest — with grades A–F, not A–D.

**7. Site percolation is not the same removal process as vein severing.** Sack et al. (2008) is the closest empirical analogue but removes veins deterministically by order and position; site percolation removes nodes uniformly at random. The paper should not present the experimental literature as validating its removal model, only as establishing that vein networks have topology-dependent damage thresholds.

**8. Leaf veins are not xylem conduits.** The r in `Resistance_4` is the width of a segmented vein — a bundle containing multiple conduits, phloem, parenchyma and fibres. The deposit's own provision of `Resistance_2` as a bundle-aware alternative is an implicit acknowledgement by the data providers that l/r⁴ may be the wrong scaling at this level of organisation. This, not the pit resistance, is the paper's deepest vulnerability, and the r²/r⁴ sensitivity analysis is the cheapest available answer to it.

**9. K_leaf itself is method-dependent.** Sack & Holbrook (2006): K_leaf varies >65-fold across species and is measured by four non-equivalent methods (HPFM, EFM, VPM, rehydration kinetics) that respectively push, evaporate, pull, and transiently rehydrate. The deposit used **EFM**. Some of the ρ = 0.187 attenuation is measurement noise in the criterion variable, not model error — worth one sentence, but not a substitute for point 2.
